## Experience and layout decision

September 13 hierarchy revision: the user authorized the six-part recommendation
after reviewing the first workspace. The earlier verification below proves its
behaviors, not acceptance of its rejected geometry. This revision is now verified
for human review with new ordinary-viewport evidence and independent review;
the [receipt](design-system-current-rebalance-workspace-evidence/index.md) owns

One implementation owner; no competing candidates. A final read-only Dark/Light
pair reviews this revision while the owner checks documentation and evidence.
Neither reviewer edits files or operates the browser. Missing review stays pending.

- A visitor first sees identity, objective state, progress and the applicable
  permissionless deadline. A connection affordance does not claim permission.
- A launcher reviews the next auction's estimate, assets and visible warnings;
  any weight prerequisite happens in the same working surface. The action stays
  in a stable operation region through wallet request, receipt and indexing.
- A returning participant sees live bids or the next required auction without
  losing overall progress. Auction number is never “of N”. Expiry/completion
  preserves the result until the user moves it into history.

The overall header owns proposal provenance and expiry together beneath the
title. Expiry is a normal-weight 14px/20px sentence, without a clock or field-like
label/value spacing. Details is the sole quiet action beside the title. Metadata
wraps beneath both on narrow widths without reducing its text size.
Details opens general Rebalance information, not the next auction's terms;
its actual 44px target has no rounded hover fill and its chevron reflects open state.
One Auction N heading and objective status own the trade
plan and launch terms together. Before launch, a roughly 60/40 body places Selling
and Buying in two stacked groups on the left and terms/access/action on the right.
There is no nested colored surface, sidebar title or second padding owner. Warnings
remain visible outside the asset disclosure without stretching unrelated grid rows.
Assets and liquidity expands at full width below this body, using established
Table/identity/value anatomy and a constrained record projection. One disclosure
owns access to the complete list; token summaries no longer toggle it secretly.
During live monitoring, chart and bids replace preparation under the same Auction N
heading. Refreshed liquidity remains explicitly estimated, not original live terms.
Rebalance so far follows the working area: cumulative progress, completed auctions,
traded value and deviation. The next target belongs to auction terms, not the
cumulative progress rail. Before any trading, cumulative context stays compact.
Phone order is heading, trade plan, terms/access/action, optional asset inspection,
then cumulative context. The editor remains in this auction's working area. History
retains its static card-color table, content axes and absence of gray row dividers.

The strongest risk is that a long expanded asset list could obscure the relationship
to launch and push cumulative context away. Keep the launch summary before expansion,
give the expanded list clear column headers/group labels, preserve its collapse
control, and inspect the full snapshot-sized list at ordinary desktop/phone heights.

Transfer from transaction work: stable action emphasis, matched 14/20 inline
facts, retained draft/operation identity and scoped recovery. Reject its dialog
width, amount objects, stepper, large outcomes and celebratory attachment.
Primary preparation and live monitoring are rendered and critiqued before the
remaining scenario variants. Visible pressure cases (not held-out): hybrid
editor at 320px, unknown live state, and two current records with independent
drafts/operations.

Rubric: (1) state/action understood without opening details; (2) restrained
hierarchy alongside history; (3) all preserved jobs reachable; (4) no fake
financial/transaction certainty; (5) same task survives narrow/wide and recovery.

## Candidate and verification boundary

Implemented in `src/views/internal/design-system/auctions-current/`, integrated
through the retained history review. All live behavior is local simulation.
The selection query is lab-local, including a guarded preview reset; browser
leave warns for pending operations, drafts and running filler. Production
router blocking remains unimplemented. A mocked connect deliberately chooses
the launcher persona, visible in the Viewer control; it does not infer real
wallet permissions. The UI's safe data gates are proposed adoption contracts.

The hybrid form uses React Hook Form/Zod with decimal strings and bigint-only
illustrative allocation. CSV is atomic and rejects incomplete/unknown/duplicate
records rather than copying production's silent partial-import behavior. Source
token identities and fallback logos are retained. Non-preset drafts and limits
survive failure, responsive changes and editor return, not reload. No amount
here is an SDK weight or transaction argument.

The bid curve is expressly schematic, as is production's; real-valued price
history is not invented. Bid details remain separately labelled illustrative.
Traded value is cumulative across auctions, with each simulated bid set counted
once. The completed view and its historical row share the same fixture result.
Current-list loading and unmatched routes never manufacture a usable record.

snapshot, explicitly not a real CMC20 holding. Limited and market-closed scenes
retain session, next-open/close, capacity and upcoming-event disclosures. These
are independent illustrative inputs, not executable quotes or cap calculations.

keyboard/touch popovers; retries remain separate, reachable buttons.
